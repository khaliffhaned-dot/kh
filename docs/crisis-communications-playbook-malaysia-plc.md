# PART A — FOUNDATIONS

## 1. Purpose, scope and how to use this playbook

### 1.1 Purpose

A crisis at a public listed company (PLC) is two crises at once: an **operational or reputational event**, and a **capital-markets event**. The first is managed with speed and empathy; the second is governed by continuous-disclosure law, insider-trading law and the Exchange's expectations of equal access to information. Most failed crisis responses in listed companies come from treating these as separate workstreams. This playbook fuses them.

It gives your team:

- a **60-minute quick-start** that works when nobody has time to read (Section 2);
- **clear decision rights** — who may say what, to whom, and in what order (Sections 6, 9);
- **Malaysia-specific disclosure mechanics** — Bursa-first disclosure, trading halts, unusual market activity (UMA) queries, insider controls (Section 9);
- **13 scenario playbooks** — from ransomware to a 3R-sensitive social media storm (Section 15);
- **ready-to-edit templates**, including Bahasa Malaysia drafts (Section 16);
- **three tabletop exercises** to test the plan (Section 17);
- a **preparedness and recovery programme** (Sections 18–19).

### 1.2 Who it is for

| Audience | Use |
|---|---|
| Crisis Management Team (CMT) | Sections 2, 6–9, 15 as live operating instructions |
| Board / Audit & Risk Committee | Sections 3, 6, 7, 9, 19 — oversight and escalation |
| Company Secretary, Legal, IR | Sections 5, 9, 16 — disclosure and regulator workflow |
| Corporate Communications / PR agency | Sections 10–14, 16 |
| Subsidiary and regional heads | Sections 2, 7, 14 — know when and how to escalate |

### 1.3 Scope and assumptions

- **Issuer type.** A company listed on Bursa Malaysia's **Main Market or ACE Market**, with subsidiaries, possibly regional operations (including Sabah and Sarawak) and a mix of institutional and retail shareholders.
- **ACE Market.** The ACE Market Listing Requirements have a parallel continuing-disclosure chapter (Chapter 9). A March 2026 ACE Market Chapter 9 Q&A document applies the same Paragraph 9.03 materiality test **[C]**. Paragraph numbers quoted in this playbook are **Main Market** numbers; confirm the ACE equivalent. ACE issuers that are still within their Sponsor period should include the Sponsor in their crisis contact tree **[V]**.
- **Cross-listed or bond-funded issuers.** If you have securities on another exchange, or sukuk/bonds with a trustee, you carry parallel disclosure obligations. Align timing so that no market receives information before another. This playbook does not cover foreign exchange rules.
- **Regulated sectors.** Banks, insurers, capital-market licensees, telcos, utilities, healthcare, food and others have sector-regulator notification duties that sit *on top of* Bursa obligations (Section 5.3).
- **Not covered.** Detailed legal analysis of any particular matter; employment-law procedure; insurance claims handling; business-continuity technical recovery (this playbook links to, but does not replace, your BCP and IT disaster-recovery plans).

### 1.4 The three-layer structure

1. **Cards** — one-page quick references (Section 2; the clock board in 2.2; severity matrix in 7.2).
2. **Procedures** — how-to for each function (Sections 6–14).
3. **Reference and tools** — scenarios, templates, exercises, directories (Sections 15–17, Appendices).

## 2. The First 60 Minutes — quick-start card

> **Print this page. Put it in the Crisis Lead's bag, the Company Secretary's desk drawer and the security desk.**

### 2.1 The sequence

| When | Do this | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **0–10 min** | **1. Verify and name it.** What exactly do we know? Who confirmed it? What do we *not* know? Write a two-line factual summary. | Person who received the alert |
| | **2. Protect people first.** Safety, containment, evacuation, isolate affected systems. Communications never delays a life-safety action. | Operations / HSE / CISO |
| | **3. Call the Core Five** (below) by phone, not email. | First recipient |
| **10–20 min** | **4. Freeze.** Legal hold on documents and messages. Pause scheduled posts, campaigns, emails. No external comment by anyone except the named spokesperson. | Legal / Comms |
| | **5. Insider lock.** Tell everyone who now knows: do **not** trade, do **not** tip (Template T6). Start the insider log. | Company Secretary |
| | **6. Start the decision log.** Time-stamp every decision and its reason (Template T3). | Scribe |
| **20–40 min** | **7. Materiality screen.** Run the Disclosure Decision Tree (Section 9.2). If it *might* be price-sensitive, treat it as price-sensitive until counsel says otherwise. | Disclosure Committee |
| | **8. Market-hours check.** Market open? If material and you cannot announce *now*, consider a **trading halt request** (Section 9.4). | CEO + Company Secretary |
| | **9. Convene the CMT** (war room — physical or virtual). | Crisis Lead |
| **40–60 min** | **10. Start the regulatory clocks** (2.2). Assign an owner to every clock. | Legal / Compliance |
| | **11. Notify the Board** — Chair and Audit & Risk Committee Chair, by phone. | Crisis Lead |
| | **12. Release the first words.** Holding statement + internal staff note, approved via the fast track (Section 8.1). **Bursa first** if the matter is material. | Head of Comms |
| | **13. Set the next update time** (every 60–120 minutes) and say so publicly. | Crisis Lead |

### 2.2 The Core Five

| # | Role | Why they are on the first call |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crisis Lead** (CEO / MD or delegate) | Single accountable decision-maker |
| 2 | **Company Secretary** | Controls Bursa LINK submissions and the statutory/regulatory record |
| 3 | **Head of Legal / General Counsel** | Privilege, legal exposure, regulator posture |
| 4 | **Head of Corporate Communications** | Statements, media, social, languages |
| 5 | **CFO / Head of Investor Relations** | Market impact, lenders, ratings, shareholder communications |

*Any one of the Core Five may declare a minimum **Level 2** (Section 7) on their own authority. Nobody needs permission to raise the alarm.*

## 3. Guiding principles

These ten principles resolve most hard calls. When in doubt, re-read them.

1. **People before reputation.** Safety, welfare and dignity of affected people come first in action *and* in order of messaging.
2. **Disclosure before dissemination.** For anything that may be material, **Bursa first** — the market must receive it through the Exchange announcement system (Bursa LINK) before, or at the same moment as, any press release, social post, analyst call or shareholder briefing. No selective disclosure **[C — equal-access principle]**.
3. **Presume disclosure.** Where there is doubt whether information is material, the presumption is in favour of disclosure. Bursa's Corporate Disclosure Guide is reported to say that the exceptions to immediate disclosure are narrow and that, in doubt, the presumption must always be in favour of disclosure **[C]**.
4. **Facts, not forecasts.** State only what is verified. Say what you know, what you don't yet know, what you are doing, and when you will update. Never speculate, never minimise, never promise "no impact" before you know.
5. **One voice, many channels.** One approved fact base; one authorised spokesperson per audience; identical facts across every language and channel.
6. **Own it, don't blame.** Acknowledge responsibility for what is yours to fix. Do not blame victims, contractors, regulators, journalists or "social media".
7. **Never say "no comment".** Say what you can, explain what you can't say yet and why (investigation, legal process, regulatory confidentiality), and commit to a time for the next update.
8. **No trading, no tipping.** Everyone who knows is an insider. Trading restrictions go out in the first hour (Section 9.8).
9. **Record everything.** The decision log is your evidence of good faith to Bursa, the Securities Commission, courts, auditors and insurers.
10. **Respect the sensitivities.** Race, religion and royalty (3R), faith practices, festive calendars and regional (including East Malaysian) identities are not "tone" issues; they are crisis multipliers (Section 4).

## 4. The Malaysian context — what changes how a crisis plays out

This section is practitioner judgement, not legal text. Use it to adapt global crisis-communications habits to local conditions.

### 4.1 Regulatory architecture in one paragraph

Bursa Malaysia Securities Berhad is the **front-line regulator** of listed issuers through the Listing Requirements, and it is the channel through which material information reaches the market. The **Securities Commission Malaysia (SC)** is the statutory regulator under the Capital Markets and Services Act 2007 (CMSA), including market-misconduct offences such as insider trading (CMSA s.188 **[C]**). Sector regulators (for example Bank Negara Malaysia, the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission, the Ministry of Health) and enforcement agencies (for example the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission and the Royal Malaysia Police) have their own powers and their own communications expectations. In a serious crisis you may deal with **four or five regulators simultaneously, each with a different clock.**

### 4.2 Ownership and shareholder structure

- Many PLCs have **concentrated ownership** — founders, families, or controlling shareholders — and many are **government-linked** or have large institutional anchors (for example the Employees Provident Fund, Permodalan Nasional Berhad, Khazanah Nasional, Kumpulan Wang Persaraan (Diperbadankan), Lembaga Tabung Haji — examples only; map your own register).
- **Implication:** informal information flows between the company, the controlling shareholder and big holders are common. In a crisis these flows create **insider-information and selective-disclosure risk**. Anything shared with a major holder that is material and undisclosed makes that holder an insider. Decide deliberately who is told what, and when (Section 9.9).
- **Retail participation** is significant in many small- and mid-cap counters (verify current figures with Bursa; none are asserted here). Retail investors use **WhatsApp, Telegram, Facebook groups and investor forums**. Rumours reach them faster than announcements. Prepare short, plain-language summaries in BM and Chinese for these audiences *after* the Bursa announcement.
- The **Minority Shareholders Watch Group (MSWG)** engages actively with listed companies and may raise questions publicly or at general meetings. Treat it as a stakeholder, not a threat.

### 4.3 Language and register

- **Bahasa Malaysia** is the national language; **English** is the language of Bursa announcements and most institutional communication; **Mandarin** and **Tamil** matter for customers, staff and community; Iban, Kadazan-Dusun and other languages matter in East Malaysia; your **migrant workforce** may speak Bahasa Indonesia, Bengali, Nepali, Burmese and others.
- **Rule:** the first public statement should be available in **English and BM** at the same time. Do not publish English first and "follow with BM later" if the issue touches the public — the delay itself becomes the story. Prepare Mandarin and Tamil within hours for consumer-facing crises.
- Use the correct **honorifics and styles** (Tan Sri, Dato' Sri, Datuk Seri, Dato', Datuk, Dr, Puan Sri, etc.) in every statement and in how spokespersons are introduced. Errors read as disrespect.

### 4.4 The 3R and community sensitivities

- **Race, Religion, Royalty (3R)** are legally and socially sensitive in Malaysia. Content that touches any of them can escalate from a corporate embarrassment to a police-report-and-political-statement event within hours. Whether any specific statement breaches a law (for example under the Sedition Act 1948 or the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998, s.233 **[V]**) is for counsel — but *comms must treat any 3R-adjacent incident as automatically Level 3 until reviewed* (Section 7).
- For food, beverage, cosmetics and pharmaceutical issuers, **halal integrity** is a trust issue as well as a compliance one.
- **Apologies** for 3R-related lapses should be prompt, sincere, specific about what was wrong and what has been done, and free of "if anyone was offended" phrasing (Scenario S12, Template T13).

### 4.5 East Malaysia and regional press

Sabah and Sarawak have their own media ecosystems, political dynamics and identities. A statement drafted in Kuala Lumpur and distributed only to Klang Valley media can look dismissive or invisible in East Malaysia. Maintain East Malaysian media contacts and, where operations are there, a regional spokesperson. Check whether your crisis affects Sabah and Sarawak differently (for example MA63-related sensitivities, local employment, indigenous community relations).

### 4.6 The calendar

- **Ramadan, Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Chinese New Year, Deepavali, Hari Gawai, Kaamatan, Christmas, Malaysia Day, school holidays** and long weekends change staff availability, media pickup, tone and public mood. A cheerful marketing post during a crisis, or an incident at a *balik kampung* peak, magnifies backlash.
- State-level holidays and, in some states, different weekends affect when officials and counterparties are reachable. Keep the Bursa market-holiday calendar and a state-by-state calendar in the war-room kit.
- Build **dual-calendar awareness** (Gregorian and Hijri, plus major festive dates) into campaign pauses and statement timing.

### 4.7 Channels and rumour dynamics

- **WhatsApp is the primary channel** for rumour spread: forwarded messages, screenshots of "internal memos", voice notes, and fake "announcements". Assume every internal message may be leaked as a screenshot.
- **TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube, Telegram, Threads and — for Chinese-speaking audiences — platforms such as Xiaohongshu** may each carry a different version of the story.
- Deepfakes, impersonated company accounts and fake announcements are credible risks (Scenario S13).

### 4.8 Politics and public institutions

Ministers, Members of Parliament, state governments and agencies may comment on a corporate crisis before the company has finished its disclosure. For GLCs and heavily regulated sectors, political attention can be immediate. Maintain a **Government and Regulatory Relations** lead who coordinates with the CEO, and make sure no ministerial or regulator conversation yields market-sensitive information to one party that hasn't been announced to the market.

## 5. Regulatory and legal landscape

> **Reminder:** [C] = corroborated by a secondary source during drafting; [V] = verify with counsel. **None of the paragraph numbers have been checked against official text.** Treat this section as a checklist of *questions for counsel*.

### 5.1 Continuous disclosure and market conduct

| # | Obligation | Source | What it means in a crisis | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Immediate public disclosure of material information** | Main LR Para 9.03(1); ACE Market LR Chapter 9 | When material information exists, announce it immediately via Bursa LINK, unless a narrow exception applies. Do not wait for the next periodic report. | **[C]** |
| 2 | **Materiality test** | Main LR Para 9.03 / Chapter 1 definition | Information is material if it is reasonably expected to have a material effect on the price, value or market activity of the issuer's securities. The definition also refers to an investor's decision on what action to take. | **[C]** for price/value/market-activity limb; **[V]** for investor-decision limb wording |
| 3 | **Exception to immediate disclosure (confidentiality)** | Main LR Para 9.05 | Narrow. | 4 | **Response to Bursa queries / unusual market activity (UMA)** | Main LR Para 9.16 | Bursa enforcement releases on Lay Hong Berhad and Tecnic Group Berhad (both 2017) describe public reprimands and fines on directors for breaches of Para 9.16(1)(a) in UMA responses. | 7 | **Directors' dealings and closed periods** | Main LR Chapter 14 (e.g. Para 14.08 notification) | Directors face restrictions on dealing before results announcements and notification duties after dealing. **Confirm the current closed-period length and scope.** | **[V]** |
| 8 | **Insider trading and tipping** | CMSA s.188 | Dealing, or communicating inside information, is a criminal offence. SC enforcement releases cite imprisonment of up to 10 years and a fine of not less than RM1 million; the SC can also pursue civil proceedings (s.201(6)). | **[C]** — verify penalty figures |
| 9 | **Financial-condition regime** | Practice Note 17 (Main Market); Guidance Note 3 (ACE) | PN17 classification arises where, among other criteria, shareholders' equity falls to 25% or less of share capital and below RM40 million (reported in a July 2026 PN17 classification). A PN17 issuer must regularise. | **[C]** for the equity trigger as applied; **[V]** for the RM40m figure in current text and for GN3 |
| 10 | **Corporate disclosure policy / governance expectations** | Main LR Chapter 9; Malaysian Code on Corporate Governance 2021 | Issuers are expected to have a corporate disclosure policy and clear spokesperson authority. | **[V]** |
| 11 | **No selective disclosure** | Main LR Para 9.08(2); limited exceptions in 9.08(3) | Material information must not go to analysts, shareholders, journalists or others before it has been fully disclosed to the public. Bursa's Q&A, as summarised in secondary sources, allows narrow exceptions (for example disclosure to advisers in due diligence under strict confidentiality, with market activity closely monitored). Ask the Company Secretary before relying on any exception. | **[C, secondary]** — verify against the current text |
| 12 | **Winding-up petitions** | Main LR Para 9.19(19)(a)(i) | Bursa's September 2026 reprimand of Ahmad Zaki Resources cites this paragraph for failing to announce winding-up petitions immediately (delays of roughly 10 to 50 days), and states that ongoing settlement talks with the petitioner do not excuse non-disclosure. Treat any petition, notice or order as a disclosure trigger. | **[C, secondary]** — verify against the current text |
| 11 | **Takeover rules** | SC Rules on Take-overs, Mergers and Compulsory Acquisitions | Rumours or leaks about offers trigger special announcement/conduct duties. Take specialist advice immediately. | **[V]** |
| 12 | **Bursa's Corporate Disclosure Guide** | Bursa Malaysia (2011 edition circulated; check for later versions) | Practical guidance on disclosure, analyst/media interaction, rumours and market-sensitive information. | **[C]** existence; **[V]** current version |

> **Why enforcement matters to comms.** The enforcement pattern is consistent: Bursa focuses less on the bad news itself than on **delayed disclosure, inaccurate or incomplete disclosure and evasive replies to Exchange queries**, and it fines individual directors. In a crisis, the way you communicate is itself a compliance event.

### 6.4 Conflicts and the "compromised leader" protocol

Pre-agree what happens if the crisis implicates the CEO, a director, the CFO, the Company Secretary or the Head of Legal:

- That person is **recused** from decisions about the matter (but may be interviewed) and the **alternate** acts.
- The **Lead Independent Director** or **ARC Chair** assumes oversight; an independent investigation (external counsel and, where relevant, forensic accountants) reports to them, not to management.
- Communications about the person use **presumption-of-innocence language** while remaining honest about measures taken (leave, suspension, restricted access).
- Document recusals in the decision log.

### 6.5 Activation protocol

1. **Trigger.** Anyone aware of a potential crisis calls the **Crisis Hotline** [number] or the Head of Comms / Company Secretary directly. *Do not rely on email or a group chat.*
2. **Core Five call** within 15 minutes; they agree an initial level (Section 7).
3. **CMT convened** — Level 3–4 within 60 minutes; Level 2 as needed.
4. **War room opened** (6.6). Scribe starts the log. First situation report (SITREP, Template T2) within 90 minutes.
5. **Cadence set:** CMT huddles every 60 minutes initially, then 2–4 hours, then twice daily. Written SITREPs at least every 4 hours at Level 3–4.
6. **Shift plan** for crises beyond 12 hours — assign relief for every role; no one runs a crisis for 30 hours without sleep.

### 6.6 War room

- **Physical room** [location, with backup in a different building], with screens, whiteboard for the clock board, power backup, printed playbook, contact sheets, and the war-room kit (Section 18.3).
- **Virtual room** — a dedicated, access-controlled channel for the CMT with **message retention switched on** (do not use ephemeral/disappearing messages for decisions). Keep the membership list tight; a former employee in the group is a leak.
- **Out-of-band channel** — if the incident is cyber, assume email, chat and VoIP may be compromised. Agree in advance an alternative (personal-device tree, phone bridge, printed phone list) and test it twice a year.
- **Rule of the room:** facts stay in the war room; opinions go to the decision log; nothing goes to social media or personal chats.

## 7. Crisis classification and escalation

### 7.1 Why levels exist

Levels set **who is in the room, how fast approvals move and how much of the Board is involved**. A level is a working assumption, reviewed every hour; upgrade quickly, downgrade slowly.

### 7.2 Severity matrix

> **The numeric thresholds below are examples. Calibrate them to your company's size, risk appetite and materiality judgement, and have the Board approve them.**

| | **Level 1 — Issue** | **Level 2 — Incident** | **Level 3 — Crisis** | **Level 4 — Critical** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Description** | Contained, local, low visibility | Notable, still manageable by a business unit with HQ support | Significant; threatens brand, licence, or share price; likely national attention | Existential or systemic; threat to the company's continuity, licence, solvency or leadership |
| **People** | Minor first-aid | Injury requiring treatment; employee-conduct complaint | Fatality or multiple serious injuries; large-scale customer harm | Multiple fatalities; widespread public harm |
| **Financial (examples — calibrate)** | < [x]% of group revenue/profit | [x–y]% | > [y]% or covenants at risk | Solvency/going-concern in question; PN17 risk |

### 7.3 Escalation triggers — "if any is true, escalate"

- A journalist, regulator, law-enforcement agency or politician has asked about it.
- An employee, customer or contractor has been killed or seriously harmed.
- Personal data of customers or staff may have left your control.
- The share price or trading volume is moving without explanation, or Bursa has contacted you.
- Allegations of corruption, fraud, accounting irregularity or misconduct involving directors or senior management.
- A major customer, lender, regulator or licensing body has expressed concern.
- The matter is on WhatsApp in a form you did not create.
- Anything touches race, religion or royalty.
- You are not sure.

## 8. Response timeline and checklists

### 8.1 The fast track for first words

To avoid the delay that turns a bad day into a bad week, pre-agree a **30-minute fast track** for holding statements:

1. Comms drafts from the pre-approved holding statement (Template T7–T12).
2. Legal and Company Secretary review (10 minutes).
3. If market-sensitive: Disclosure Committee decides on Bursa announcement first.
4. Release in **English and BM** together.
5. Log the release time and wording.

### 8.2 Phase checklists

#### Phase 1 — Stabilise (0–1 hour)
- [ ] Safety/containment actions under way
- [ ] Two-line verified fact summary written
- [ ] Core Five reached; Crisis Lead named
- [ ] Legal hold issued; scheduled content paused
- [ ] Insider log started; do-not-trade notice sent (T6)
- [ ] Decision log started (T3)
- [ ] Initial materiality screen done (Section 9.2)
- [ ] Regulatory clocks identified and owners assigned (Section 2.3)
- [ ] Spokesperson identified; staff told "refer all queries to Comms"

#### Phase 2 — Assess and announce (1–4 hours)
- [ ] CMT convened; war room operational
- [ ] Facts verified by two sources before use externally
- [ ] Bursa announcement and/or halt decision made and logged
- [ ] Holding statement released (EN + BM); staff note released
- [ ] Board Chair/ARC Chair informed; broader Board briefed as required
- [ ] Media-query log open; first responses sent (acknowledge, give time of next update)
- [ ] Social and media monitoring live with a named escalation owner
- [ ] Key customers, lenders and partners given a short heads-up **after** the Bursa announcement if the matter is material
- [ ] Insurers notified; external advisers engaged

#### Phase 3 — Sustain (4–24 hours)
- [ ] SITREP issued; next update time published
- [ ] Facts refreshed; Q&A updated (Section 11)
- [ ] Regulators briefed as required; filings made
- [ ] Employee briefing/town hall held; managers equipped with talking points
- [ ] Analyst/investor communications prepared for release **after** Bursa announcement
- [ ] Rumour log reviewed; corrections issued only through official channels
- [ ] Shift roster in place; fatigue managed
- [ ] Share-price and trading-volume watch with CFO/IR

#### Phase 4 — Consolidate (24–72 hours)
- [ ] Root-cause work commissioned with clear independence where needed
- [ ] Follow-up Bursa announcements made as facts develop (an "update" announcement every time the material picture changes)
- [ ] Support for affected people (victims, customers, staff, families) visible and measurable
- [ ] Stakeholder map reviewed; any missed group contacted
- [ ] Board decision on any extraordinary measures (leaves, independent review, leadership change)
- [ ] Press conference or interview scheduled if warranted (Section 12.5)

#### Phase 5 — Stabilise and investigate (Days 4–14)
- [ ] Independent findings schedule communicated
- [ ] Regulatory submissions and follow-ups tracked
- [ ] Customer/supplier remediation under way
- [ ] Employee pulse check and counselling support
- [ ] Reputation metrics tracked (Section 19.3)

#### Phase 6 — Recover (Weeks 2–12)
- [ ] 30-60-90 rebuild plan approved (Section 19.2)

- [ ] Insurance recoveries and legal exposures tracked

#### Phase 7 — Learn (within 30 days of de-escalation)
- [ ] After-action review completed (Template T17)

- [ ] Board receives the review

### 8.3 Fatigue and well-being

Crisis teams make worse decisions after 18+ hours. Rotate decision-makers, schedule meals and rest, and provide counselling access to team members who have faced traumatic events (fatalities, threats, online harassment). Include the families of crisis-team members in your thinking: staff may be doxxed or harassed.

# PART C — COMMUNICATE

## 9. Disclosure and capital-markets playbook (Bursa-first)

This is the section that distinguishes a PLC playbook from a generic one. It covers what you must tell the market, how and when, and how you keep information under control until you do.

### 9.1 Core rules in plain language

1. **If it is material, announce it immediately through Bursa.** Not "after the press conference", not "after the board meeting next week", not "when we know more". The obligation is "immediate" disclosure **[C]**; in practice that means as soon as the facts allow an accurate announcement, and an interim announcement of what you know is better than silence. *(That practical reading is the drafter's, not a quotation — confirm how the Listing Requirements define "immediately" **[V]**.)*
2. **Bursa first, everyone else second.** Media, analysts, lenders, customers, employees and social media hear it at the same time as or after the market. The practical sequence for a material event is: *Bursa LINK announcement → press release / website / social → staff note → analyst and investor calls → stakeholder letters.* The exception is life-safety instruction, which is never delayed.
3. **The exception to immediate disclosure is narrow and short-lived.** Companies have been able to withhold material information that is in a state of flux, but Bursa enforcement releases state that this right ceases when unusual market activity suggests a leak **[C]**. Treat any leak signal — UMA, rumour, press query, social chatter, unusual volume — as the end of the confidentiality window.
4. **Do not use "negotiations in progress" as a shield.** Bursa has stated that unpublished material information, including matters at the negotiation or discussion stage, must be disclosed when the exception ends **[C — Lay Hong 2017 release]**.
5. **Accuracy beats speed — but speed has a ceiling.** A short, accurate holding announcement ("the company is aware of X; investigation under way; further announcement to follow") is better than a perfect one delivered after rumours have moved the stock.
6. **Say the same thing everywhere.** Bursa announcement, press release, website, social posts and scripts must be word-consistent on facts, numbers and dates.
7. **Update when the picture changes.** Each material development triggers a new announcement; do not let the market's understanding drift from the facts.

### 9.3 Writing a good Bursa announcement

**Standard content (adapt to the incident type):**

1. **Heading** — Company name and a plain, specific subject. Avoid euphemism.
2. **Opening** — "The Board of Directors of [Company] wishes to announce that…" followed by the core fact in one or two sentences.
3. **What happened** — date, time, place, nature; how discovered.
4. **Who/what is affected** — people, operations, customers, assets, data.
5. **Financial and operational impact** — quantify where possible; if not yet quantifiable, say so and say when you expect to update.
6. **Actions taken** — containment, authorities notified, investigations commissioned.
7. **Next steps** — what will be announced next and roughly when.
8. **Compliance statement** — a reference to the relevant Listing Requirements paragraph where your counsel recommends one.
9. **Authorisation** — "This announcement is made pursuant to Paragraph 9.03 of the Main Market Listing Requirements" (or the ACE Market equivalent) **[V]**, and board/authorised-person details.

**Style rules:** plain English; no adjectives of reassurance ("minimal", "negligible", "robust") unless you can prove them; no forward-looking statements without a stated basis; no speculation on cause or fault; no promotional language; do not "bury" the lead behind background.

**Mechanics (confirm with your Company Secretary):** announcements are submitted through Bursa LINK in the appropriate announcement type with a free-text narrative **[V]**. Keep **at least two authorised submitters** on file with tested credentials and a manual back-up route agreed with Bursa, because the day you need to announce is the day the primary submitter is on a flight.

### 9.4 Trading halts (suspension on issuer request)

**When to consider a halt**

- Material information exists and **cannot be announced immediately** in an accurate, orderly manner while the market is open.
- A leak has occurred and the market is trading on rumour, but the facts are not yet confirmed enough to announce.
- A major transaction, default, regulatory action or board event is being finalised.

**How (indicative; confirm with counsel and Bursa)**

1. Telephone your Bursa relationship contact **before** filing; explain the basis.
2. File the request under Practice Note 2 (Requests for Suspension) of the Main Market Listing Requirements (press examples cite sub-paragraphs 3.1(a) and 3.1(b)) **[C]**.
3. The request states the reason and the proposed period. Press examples show suspension from market open until 5 pm on the following market day pending a "material announcement" **[C]**; Bursa decides.
4. Prepare the announcement to be released within the halt period; resume of trading follows Bursa's process.
5. Brief IR, the Board and the spokespersons: a halt is itself a signal. Prepare a one-line holding message: *"The company has requested a trading suspension pending an announcement. We will announce as soon as we are able to."*

**Do not** request a halt as a way to buy time for a decision that you could have made. A halt that expires without an announcement damages credibility and may attract questions.

### 9.5 Unusual market activity (UMA) queries and rumours

**What a UMA query asks.** Typically: whether the company is aware of any unannounced corporate development, matters under negotiation, any rumour or report, or any other explanation for the trading pattern, and whether it is complying with Para 9.03 **[C]**.

**Protocol**

1. **Treat it as a Level 3 trigger**, even if the answer will turn out benign.
2. **Read the query's deadline** and put it on the clock board.
3. **Do real due enquiry** — not a "tick-box". Ask each director, key management personnel, the heads of major subsidiaries, relevant advisers and the controlling shareholder (through the right channel) whether they are aware of anything that might explain the activity. Record who was asked, when, and their answer.
4. **Check the last 30 days** of internal events: board papers, term sheets, regulator letters, rating-agency contacts, major customer or lender discussions, incident reports.
5. **Check for leaks**: media queries, social chatter, unusual calls from analysts or brokers.
6. **Draft the reply** with Legal and Company Secretary. State what you did to enquire; then state, accurately, whether there is anything to disclose. If there *is* undisclosed material information, the reply must disclose it (or, if it exists but needs to be announced simultaneously, announce in the same release).
7. **Board endorsement** before release **[C]**.
8. **Keep the file**: the enquiry record is your defence if the reply is later challenged.

> **Why this matters.** Bursa has publicly reprimanded issuers and fined their directors (examples carried in 2017 enforcement releases for Lay Hong Berhad and Tecnic Group Berhad) for breaches connected with their UMA responses **[C]**. A glib or inaccurate UMA reply is among the highest-penalty communications errors a PLC can make.

**Rumours and press reports that are not a UMA query.** If a media report or market rumour concerns material information:

- If it is **true or substantially true** → announce.
- If it is **false or materially inaccurate** and may mislead the market → consider a prompt clarification announcement; discuss with Bursa and counsel **[V]**.
- If **partly true** → announce the true part, correct the rest.
- Do **not** say "we do not comment on market rumours" if the rumour concerns material information and trading is active.

| Step | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Safety and containment | Immediately |
| 1 | Board Chair / ARC Chair informed | ≤ 1 hour |
| 2 | Halt decision (if needed) | Before announcement, market open |
| 3 | **Bursa LINK announcement** | As soon as accurate |
| 4 | Press release (EN + BM), website, corporate social accounts | Same moment as step 3 or immediately after |
| 5 | Staff note and manager talking points | Within minutes of step 3 |
| 6 | Key institutional shareholders, lenders, rating agencies, regulators — "heads-up" calls limited to *public* information | Within hours |
| 7 | Customers/partners/suppliers affected | Within hours, targeted |
| 8 | Analyst briefing / investor call (public information only) | After step 3; same day if possible |
| 9 | Retail shareholder explainers (BM, Chinese, English), Q&A on website | Within 24 hours |
| 10 | Follow-up announcement when facts change | As needed |

**Life-safety and legal-notice exceptions:** evacuation notices, customer safety warnings (for example "stop using product X"), and statutory notifications to regulators or affected individuals may go before the Bursa announcement when delay would increase harm or breach a legal deadline. Where that happens, notify Bursa at the same time and announce at the earliest practicable moment.

### 9.7 Periodic reporting during and after a crisis

- Quarterly and annual deadlines still apply **[C]** (quarterly within two months of quarter-end; annual report within four months of financial year-end).
- A crisis may affect **recognition, impairment, provisions, contingent liabilities and going-concern assessments**. Involve the CFO and external auditors early. Audit-related events (qualified opinions, going-concern emphasis, auditor resignation) are disclosure events and can have regulatory consequences, including under PN17/GN3 **[V]**.
- Align crisis messaging with the numbers: statements saying "no material impact" followed by a big impairment destroy credibility.

### 9.8 Controlling inside information — the "no trading" machinery

**Legal backdrop.** Insider trading and communicating inside information are criminal offences under the CMSA (s.188), with imprisonment of up to ten years and fines of not less than RM1 million reported in SC releases **[C — verify figures]**; the SC may also bring civil proceedings **[C]**.

**Steps (first hour)**

1. **Open the insider log** (Template T5): name, role, time they learned, what they know, how they were told.
2. **Send the do-not-trade and confidentiality notice** (Template T6) to everyone on the log, with a confirmation reply.
3. **Pause corporate dealings**: share buy-backs, ESOS/ESS exercises or grants if within company control, private placements and other equity issuance, and any scheduled director/employee dealings — until the information is public (confirm each with counsel) **[V]**.
4. **Directors and key management:** remind them of Chapter 14 notification duties and any closed-period restrictions; confirm the current rules with the Company Secretary **[V]**.
5. **Advisers:** confirm that external advisers (law firms, PR agency, auditors) are under confidentiality undertakings and are also on the log.
6. **Need-to-know:** the smaller the circle, the smaller the leak risk — and the smaller the number of people you must prove did not trade.
7. **Leak response:** if a leak is suspected, treat it as the end of the confidentiality window (Section 9.1 rule 3).

### 9.9 Dealing with controlling and major shareholders

- The controlling shareholder, GLIC anchors and other major holders **do not** get earlier access to material undisclosed information as a courtesy.
- If you must consult a major shareholder to take a decision (for example a rights issue, restructuring or board change), do so under written confidentiality with a clear understanding that they become insiders, restrict their dealings, and keep the list.
- Where the controlling shareholder wants to speak publicly, coordinate: their statement can itself be market-sensitive and may require disclosure by the company.

### 9.10 Investor relations in a crisis

- **Quiet period.** Pause analyst meetings, roadshows and one-on-ones unless they are consistent with public information. Use scripted, pre-cleared material.
- **After announcement:** hold an analyst briefing or call within 24 hours; publish the transcript or key points on the website for equal access.
- **Q&A discipline:** answer from public information. "We can't comment beyond our announcement" is acceptable; guessing is not.
- **Guidance:** do not issue earnings guidance or forecasts while you are unable to quantify the impact. If you must retract prior guidance, do so through Bursa.
- **Sell-side and buy-side:** record all contacts in an IR log. A fund manager calling at 7 am asking "is it true that…" gets the same answer as everyone: refer to the announcement.
- **Retail:** short explainers in BM and Chinese (and English), plain language, published on the website and shared with retail-facing platforms **after** the Bursa announcement.
- **Proxy advisers and MSWG:** proactive outreach with the same public information; anticipate AGM/EGM questions.

### 9.11 Lenders, trustees, rating agencies, auditors

| Counterparty | Why | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Lenders and facility agents** | Covenant breaches, material-adverse-change and event-of-default clauses | CFO and Legal review every facility agreement within hours; notify as required; seek waivers early |
| **Sukuk/bond trustees and holders** | Disclosure and default provisions in trust deeds | Review trust deed; align timing with Bursa announcement |
| **Rating agencies** (for example RAM Ratings, MARC Ratings — as applicable to your issuer) | Rating action or outlook change | CFO leads; only public information unless under proper confidentiality |
| **External auditors** | Financial statement impact; audit opinions; independence | ARC Chair and CFO brief early; auditors may need to be consulted before public statements about numbers |
| **Insurers and brokers** | Policy notification conditions | Notify promptly; use panel vendors where policy requires |

### 9.12 General meetings and special situations

- If a crisis arises before an AGM/EGM, brief the Chair on likely questions; prepare a "statement at AGM" with the same facts as the last announcement; ensure responses to shareholders are consistent with what the market has been told.
- If the crisis involves a **take-over approach, contested vote, activist campaign or boardroom dispute**, engage specialist advisers immediately and follow the SC take-over rules **[V]**.
- If **the Exchange or SC contacts you** at any hour, the Company Secretary and Legal lead the response; do not rely on PR to manage regulator contact.

## 11. Messaging framework and Q&A

### 11.1 The Five-Line Core Statement

Every first statement answers five questions, in this order:

| Line | Purpose | Example frame |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Acknowledge** | What happened — verified facts only | "[Company] confirms that [fact] occurred at [place] on [date/time]." |
| **2. Care** | Who is affected and how we are helping | "Our first priority is [people]. We are [support actions]." |
| **3. Action** | What we are doing now and who is involved | "We have [contained/stopped/notified]. [Authority] is involved." |
| **4. Accountability** | What we will find out and report | "We will establish the cause. We will share what we learn." |
| **5. Next** | When and where the next update comes | "We will update at [time] on [channel]." |

### 11.2 Message hierarchy

- **Core narrative (one sentence).** The one thing we want a tired journalist or a worried investor to remember.
- **Three proof points.** Facts that support the narrative — numbers, actions, independent verifications.
- **Audience-specific adaptations.** Employees (job security and safety), customers (service and safety), investors (financial and governance), regulators (compliance and cooperation), communities (impact and care).

### 11.3 Language rules

**Do**
- Short sentences, active voice, concrete verbs.
- Name the problem. Use the word people already use.
- Apologise when responsibility is clear and the apology is sincere.
- Show empathy before explanation.
- Give next-update times.

**Don't**
- "No comment."
- "Isolated incident" unless you can prove it.
- "Minimal impact" or "no material impact" without evidence — and never before you've done the materiality assessment.
- Blame individuals, victims, regulators, journalists or social media.
- Speculate on cause, fault or legal outcome.
- Use legalese for the public statement.
- Make promises you can't keep ("it will never happen again").
- Name victims before next of kin have been informed.
- Release anything that could be read as a forward-looking financial statement without proper basis.

### 11.4 The hard-questions bank (prepare answers *with Legal* before you need them)

| # | Likely question | Answer principle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What exactly happened? | Verified facts; say what is unknown |
| 2 | When did you first know? | Honest timeline; discrepancies destroy trust |
| 3 | Why did it take so long to tell us? | Own the timeline; explain what was verified when; show you disclosed promptly once material |
| 4 | Is anyone hurt / is anyone's data lost? | Precise; never "no evidence" unless forensics support |
| 5 | Who is responsible? | Investigation will determine; accept responsibility for what is clearly yours |
| 6 | Did the Board / CEO know? | Facts only; if unknown, say review under way and by whom |
| 7 | Will the CEO / directors resign? | Governance principle: Board will act on findings; avoid pre-judging |
| 8 | Is the company still solvent / is dividend safe? | Only what is public; avoid guidance you cannot back |
| 9 | Will jobs be lost? | Honest, humane; no promises you cannot keep |
| 10 | Are regulators investigating? | Confirm that you are cooperating; avoid detail beyond what is public |
| 11 | Is this a cover-up? | Point to transparency measures, independent review, decision timeline |
| 12 | Why did the share price move before the announcement? | Do not speculate; reference the UMA reply and the announcement timeline |
| 13 | Is the product / service safe now? | Specific safeguards; independent verification if possible |
| 14 | What compensation will affected people receive? | Concrete process; do not prejudge amounts |
| 15 | Is the problem bigger than you've said? | "We will tell you as soon as we know, through Bursa and our website" |
| 16 | Have you paid / will you pay a ransom? | Per Board decision and legal advice; avoid operational detail that helps attackers |
| 17 | Is this connected to [politician / competitor / shareholder]? | No speculation; stick to facts |
| 18 | Have you spoken to the Minister / MACC / police? | Confirm contact appropriately; no detail beyond what is public |
| 19 | What are you doing to prevent recurrence? | Specific commitments with dates |
| 20 | How can I trust you now? | Behaviour over words: independent review, published milestones |

## 12. Media playbook

### 12.1 Media landscape (illustrative — maintain your own list)

| Segment | Examples (illustrative, not exhaustive) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wire & national agency** | Bernama, Reuters, Bloomberg | Fast; others pick up |
| **English business & general** | The Edge, The Star, New Straits Times, Malay Mail, Free Malaysia Today, The Business Times (SG), Nikkei Asia | Influence investors |
| **Bahasa Malaysia** | Berita Harian, Harian Metro, Sinar Harian, Kosmo!, Utusan Malaysia | Reach mass audiences and Malay-speaking stakeholders |
| **Chinese-language** | Sin Chew Daily, China Press, Nanyang Siang Pau, Oriental Daily | Reach Chinese-speaking investors and consumers |
| **Tamil-language** | Tamil Nesan, Makkal Osai and others | Community reach |
| **Broadcast & digital** | Astro Awani, TV3, BFM, Malaysiakini, CNA | Live coverage and fast cycles |
| **East Malaysia** | Borneo Post, Daily Express (Sabah), Utusan Borneo and regional broadcasters | Treat as first-tier in East Malaysia crises |

### 12.2 Media operating procedures

1. **Single door.** All media queries to Comms. Staff are told: "Refer all enquiries to [name, number]. Do not respond, do not post."
2. **Log every query** (Template T19): outlet, journalist, time, question, deadline, owner, response, outcome.
3. **Acknowledge in 15–30 minutes.** "We have your query; we will respond by [time]." Silence breeds speculation.
4. **Fact-check before replying.** Two sources for any fact. If you cannot confirm, say "we are verifying".
5. **Bursa-first on material items.** Do not give a journalist information that is material and undisclosed. If a journalist already has it, treat as a leak (Section 9.1).
6. **Consistency.** Same facts to all outlets, in all languages. Offer exclusives only if you are comfortable the exclusive does not become selective disclosure.
7. **On the record by default.** "Off the record" and "background" arrangements are agreed in advance, explicitly, and only by experienced people. Assume anything said can be quoted.
8. **Correct errors fast and politely.** Send the source document or the Bursa announcement; request a correction; avoid public fights.
9. **Legal letters** (demand, right of reply) are sent only on advice and with the Crisis Lead's approval.
10. **Premises.** Security briefed on media, filming and drones. Designate a media area away from sensitive operations and bereaved families.

### 12.3 Statement format

Standard press statement (Template T7–T12): headline, 3–5 short paragraphs following the Five-Line Core Statement, spokesperson quote, background boilerplate, media contact. Issue in English and BM together; Mandarin and Tamil within hours where relevant.

### 12.4 Interviews and doorstops

- **Prepare:** three key messages, five hard questions (11.4), and bridging phrases.
- **Bridging:** "What matters most right now is…", "I can't speculate on that, but what I can tell you is…", "That's a fair question; here's what we know today…"
- **Never** improvise on legal liability, cause, share price or the personal circumstances of those involved.
- **Avoid doorstops** (impromptu corridor interviews). A podium, a prepared statement and a time limit are your protection.
- **Photos and video:** use the company's own footage where appropriate; do not allow staging of victims or families.

### 12.5 Press conference run-sheet (Template T19 contains the full version)

1. Bursa announcement made **before** the conference.
2. Venue controlled; backdrop neutral; language support (BM, English, others as needed) arranged.
3. Lead spokesperson delivers a 3–5 minute statement; technical expert on hand.
4. Q&A limited to 20–30 minutes; moderator ensures East Malaysia and non-English media get questions.
5. Press kit: statement, fact sheet, timeline, Q&A (public information only), contacts.
6. Debrief within 30 minutes; log what was said; correct any slip immediately (and if the slip is market-sensitive, consider a Bursa clarification).

### 12.6 Media monitoring

Set up real-time monitoring across English, BM, Chinese and Tamil press, broadcast, and social platforms. Assign an analyst to flag: new facts, errors, tone shifts, politician/regulator comments, East Malaysia coverage, and any content that touches 3R. Report to the CMT hourly in the first 24 hours.

## 13. Digital and social media

### 13.1 Monitoring and triage

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| New fact surfaces that we have not confirmed | Verify, log, brief CMT |
| Factual error spreading | Correct once, on official channels, with the source |
| Hostile but legitimate criticism | Do not argue; address substance through official statements |
| Harassment or doxxing of staff | Support staff; report to platform; Legal on police report |
| Scam, impersonation, fake announcement or deepfake | Warn publicly; report to platform; escalate to Legal/CISO (Scenario S13) |
| 3R-sensitive content | Immediate Level 3; Legal + senior leadership |
| Politicians/regulators commenting | Notify Government Relations; align statements |

### 13.2 Operating rules

- **Pause** all scheduled and automated posts, ads and campaigns across all accounts at activation.
- **Pin** the official statement and link to the dedicated crisis page (the "dark site", Section 18.2).
- **Respond in the language of the post** wherever possible.
- **Official channels only**: corporate accounts do not reply to individual posts with argument; use the pinned statement and the hotline.
- **No deleting** of existing company posts without Legal approval (evidence and perception).
- **Platform reporting** (Online Safety Act context): use user-reporting routes for fraud, impersonation and genuinely harmful content **[C]**; do not file takedown requests against legitimate criticism.
- **Ads:** consider pausing paid media; consider search-ad buys for "[company] + incident" to point to official facts.
- **WhatsApp:** prepare broadcast-list copy for staff, agents, dealers and retail investors; do not pretend that a company message won't be forwarded.
- **Scheduled posts after the crisis:** resume only on explicit CMT approval, with tone checks, and avoid festive or celebratory content for an appropriate period.

### 13.3 Employee social media

Remind employees (Template T15) not to post about the incident, speculate, share internal documents or respond to journalists, and provide them with official facts they *may* share. Do not threaten; explain. Staff who see inaccurate content should send it to Comms.

### 13.4 Dark site and crisis page

Build in advance (Section 18.2) a ready-to-activate web page with: statement, timeline, FAQs, contact information, links to Bursa announcements, safety instructions, and updates feed. Host independently from your main site if possible so that a cyber incident or traffic surge does not take it down.

## 14. Internal communications

### 14.1 Principles

- Employees are **your first stakeholders and your most credible voice**, and also your biggest leak risk.
- Employees hear it from you **no later than the public** — ideally at the same moment as the Bursa announcement for material events; earlier for safety instructions. (Employees may be insiders: remind them of the do-not-trade rules.)
- **Managers are the channel.** Equip them with a 5-bullet talking-points card (Template T15) within an hour of the public statement.
- Include **contractors, outsourced staff and migrant workers**; use the languages they actually read.

### 14.2 Content

- What happened and what we know; what we don't know yet.
- What we are doing.
- What they should do (safety, work arrangements, how to refer calls).
- What they must *not* do (post, speculate, trade).
- How to raise concerns (including the whistleblowing channel); non-retaliation assurance.
- Next update time.

### 14.3 Channels

Email, intranet, SMS, WhatsApp broadcast via managers, notice boards for operations without desktop access, town halls (in person and streamed), and a staff hotline. Use a written note *plus* a live briefing: a note alone feels cold, a briefing alone isn't recorded.

### 14.4 Leaks and discipline

Assume anything circulated internally can be leaked. Draft internal notes as if they will be read aloud on television. Where leaking is serious, handle through HR and Legal; do not discipline in ways that look like retaliation against whistleblowers (Whistleblower Protection Act 2010 **[V]**).

### 14.5 Staff support

Counselling, family liaison, flexible working, and clarity on pay and leave. After fatalities or violent events, plan memorials and leave sensitively and in consultation with the families and community.

### S1 — Cyber attack, ransomware, data breach

**Triggers:** system outage; ransom note; unusual outbound data traffic; notification from a third party/law enforcement/security researcher; data posted on leak sites; customer complaints about scam calls referencing your data.

**First 60 minutes**
1. CISO/CIO isolates affected systems and engages the **retained incident-response firm** and **cyber insurer's hotline** (policy terms often require approved vendors and prompt notice **[V]**).
2. Move the CMT to the **out-of-band channel** (Section 6.6). Assume email and chat may be monitored.
3. Legal hold; instruct staff not to wipe or "clean" machines.
4. Disclosure Committee runs the materiality screen: operational outage, data volume/sensitivity, customer impact, regulatory exposure, extortion.
5. Prepare a holding statement (T9) and a customer-safety message (scam warnings, password resets).

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** assess materiality early; outage of core operations, loss of customer data or an extortion demand can each be price-sensitive. In March 2026 Bursa Malaysia itself publicly stated that cyber incidents at several brokers were contained to the brokers' internal systems, with no impact on its own networks or trading platforms and no evidence of unauthorised trading — a useful model for the kind of structured statement (containment, scope, absence/presence of financial loss, steps taken) that regulators and investors want **[C — news report]**.
- **PDPA:** notifiable personal data breach — PDPC within 72 hours (as soon as practicable); affected individuals no later than 7 days after PDPC notification; late-notice explanation required **[C]**. Prepare the PDPC form and the individual-notice template in advance.
- **Cyber Security Act 2024:** if you are a designated NCII entity — immediate electronic notice to NACSA and your sector lead; 6-hour and 14-day reports **[C]**.
- **Financial sector:** BNM RMiT incident notification and customer communication duties **[V]**.
- **Law enforcement:** PDRM commercial-crime/cyber units and national CERT-type bodies as advised by counsel/the IR firm **[V]**.
- **Ransom:** a Board decision after legal advice (sanctions, anti-money-laundering, insurance, law-enforcement guidance) **[V]**; never delegate to IT or Comms.

**Priority stakeholders:** affected customers/employees; regulators; Bursa/market; insurers; critical partners whose systems connect to yours.

**Message pillars:** "We detected… We isolated… We engaged independent experts… We have informed the authorities… Here is what you should do… We will inform you directly if your data is affected… We will update at…"

**Pitfalls:** declaring "no evidence of data theft" before forensics can support it; sharing technical detail that assists the attacker; going quiet on staff (leading to rumours on WhatsApp); letting the CISO speak publicly without media training; promising full restoration dates you cannot meet; conflating "contained" with "resolved".

**Prepare in advance:** retained incident-response firm; cyber-insurance panel; tested out-of-band channel; pre-drafted customer notices (EN/BM); PDPC form data pre-collected (controller details, DPO contact); data map showing where personal data lives; legal view on ransom policy; a "notification matrix" mapping data type → regulator → clock.

---

### S2 — Workplace fatality, major accident, environmental release

**Triggers:** fatality or serious injury at plant/site/construction; fire, explosion, structural failure; chemical leak; spill; transport accident; community complaints.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Emergency response; BOMBA/ambulance/police as required; secure the scene.
2. **Next of kin** are contacted personally by HR/senior leader — *before* any public naming. For foreign workers, notify the relevant embassy/consulate through the proper channel **[V]**.
3. HSE head notifies DOSH (JKKP) — immediately by the quickest means; written report within 7 days **[C — secondary]**; DOE for environmental releases per licence **[V]**.
4. Preserve the scene and records; Legal hold; cooperate with authorities.
5. Holding statement (T8), staff note, hotline for families.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** assess materiality — fatalities, plant shutdown, licence suspension, legal liabilities, insurance and reputational impact. For significant sites, treat as potentially material from the outset; announce facts and impact when verified.
- **PERKESO/SOCSO:** employment-injury processes **[V]**.
- **Insurers:** immediate notice.
- **Authorities:** DOSH, DOE, PDRM, BOMBA, local authority — one liaison person per authority.

**Priority stakeholders:** families; injured; colleagues on site; unions; authorities; community; customers dependent on the site; Bursa/market.

**Message pillars:** condolence and care first; cooperation with authorities; facts only; stop-work/safety review; commitment to learn; what support families receive; when the next update is.

**Pitfalls:** naming victims before families are told; implying blame on the victim or contractor; photos of the scene shared by staff; spokesperson speculating on cause; proceeding with marketing or celebrations; restarting operations before authorities permit; ignoring migrant-worker families who may be abroad.

**Prepare in advance:** family liaison protocol; staff trained for next-of-kin notification; embassy contact list; relationships with local DOSH/BOMBA offices; site holding statements; media access plan for remote sites; Sabah/Sarawak logistics.

---

### S3 — Product safety, recall, contamination, halal-integrity concern

**Triggers:** consumer illness reports; regulator notice; test failures; supplier contamination; foreign object; mislabelling; halal certification challenge; viral photo/video of a defect.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Quality/QA freezes affected batches; identifies scope; decision-maker for **stop-sale / recall** named.
2. Regulator route: Ministry of Health (food safety, NPRA for pharmaceuticals), Medical Device Authority, KPDN, and for halal issues the relevant halal certifying authority (JAKIM or state religious authorities) **[V]**.
3. Customer-safety message (T10) — what product, what lot, what to do, how to get a refund — in BM, English, Chinese, Tamil.
4. Retail, e-commerce and distributor notices; pull from shelves; marketplace listings removed.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** recalls can be material by cost, brand, customer concentration or regulatory consequence.
- **Halal:** a halal-integrity concern is both a compliance issue and an issue of public trust; engage the relevant authority at once; avoid defensive language.
- **Insurance:** product recall policy conditions **[V]**.

**Priority stakeholders:** consumers; retailers; regulators; religious authorities where relevant; employees; suppliers.

**Message pillars:** safety first; clear identification of affected products; how to get refund/replacement; cooperation with authorities; what has changed.

**Pitfalls:** minimising ("isolated"); partial recalls that expand later; slow or hard-to-find refund process; blaming suppliers publicly; not translating notices.

**Prepare in advance:** traceability systems; recall plan with regulator contacts; consumer hotline; retailer contact tree; halal-assurance documentation and authority contacts; pre-drafted recall notices (EN/BM/Chinese/Tamil — native-speaker reviewed).

---

### S4 — Accounting irregularity, restatement, auditor issue, going-concern doubt

**Triggers:** whistleblower report; internal audit finding; auditor query or resignation; regulator enquiry; short-seller allegation; discovery of unrecorded liabilities or fictitious receivables.

**First 60 minutes**
1. **ARC Chair** assumes oversight; matter goes to a confidential workstream; **management involved in the area is walled off**.
2. Legal retains forensic accountants/investigators reporting to the ARC (not to management). Privilege approach confirmed with counsel **[V]**.
3. Disclosure Committee runs materiality screening, with the **auditors** consulted as appropriate.
4. Stop all financial guidance and IR meetings; trading restriction on everyone with knowledge (Section 9.8).
5. Prepare a holding statement (T11/T12) that confirms an independent review without prejudging findings.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** accounting irregularities, auditor resignation/removal, qualified opinion, going-concern emphasis and delayed reporting are all potentially disclosable or consequential events; confirm the exact paragraphs **[V]**. Reporting deadlines still run (quarterly within two months, annual within four months) **[C]**.
- **PN17/GN3:** assess whether equity or going-concern positions could trigger classification **[C/V]**.
- **SC:** possible enquiries or investigation; coordinate through Legal.
- **Auditors:** their reporting duties may force timeline decisions.

**Priority stakeholders:** Board/ARC; auditors; Bursa/SC; lenders; institutional investors; employees in finance; media.

**Message pillars:** independent review, commitment to transparency, protecting stakeholders, timetable for updates; no conclusions until findings.

**Pitfalls:** early denials; reassurance before facts; management-led investigations of themselves; leaks to selected analysts; "temporary" cash-flow explanations that conflict with later findings.

**Prepare in advance:** whistleblowing policy and independent channel; ARC protocols for investigations; panel of forensic advisers; template language for "independent review announced"; clear delegations for auditor contact.

---

### S5 — Corruption allegation, MACC investigation, arrest or remand of a director or executive

**Triggers:** MACC raid or call-up; arrest/remand; media exposé; whistleblower complaint; foreign enforcement agency enquiry.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Legal manages interaction with the authorities; staff are told to be polite, cooperate within the law, and **call Legal immediately**; no destruction of documents.
2. **Compromised-leader protocol** (Section 6.4): recuse implicated persons; interim arrangements; ARC Chair/Lead INED oversight.
3. Check **s.17A MACC Act** posture: is the organisation exposed through an associated person; are adequate procedures (TRUST) documented **[C]**.
4. Disclosure Committee runs materiality screening, including implications for licences, customers, financing and board composition.
5. Holding statement (T11/T12): acknowledge awareness, state cooperation, avoid commenting on the merits, affirm presumption of innocence and company values.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** arrest of a director/executive or investigation of the company can be price-sensitive; assess promptly and announce facts; update as developments occur.
- **Directors' duties and board composition:** consider recusal, leave, or suspension; check constitution and Listing Requirements for implications of the director's status **[V]**.
- **Whistleblowers:** protect; never try to identify **[V]**.
- **Lenders/customers/government contracts:** check termination/debarment clauses.

**Priority stakeholders:** Bursa/market; regulators; Board; lenders; key customers/government counterparties; employees.

**Message pillars:** cooperating fully; independent review; zero tolerance for corruption; interim governance measures; presumption of innocence; commitment to update.

**Pitfalls:** attacking the authority; defending an individual publicly before facts; hiding behind "personal matter"; ignoring the corporate-liability dimension; letting an implicated person speak for the company.

**Prepare in advance:** documented adequate-procedures framework (TRUST) with evidence; dawn-raid protocol; delegated authority and alternates; regular training; whistleblowing channel tested.

---

### S6 — Short-seller report, market rumour, UMA query, take-over approach

**Triggers:** published short report; unusual price/volume; Bursa UMA query; news or social rumours about a deal, results or defaults; unsolicited approach by a potential acquirer; activist letter.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Level 3 immediately. Disclosure Committee, external counsel, IR and Comms meet.
2. **Fact-check the allegations** line by line with the relevant business and finance leads. Sort into *true*, *partly true*, *false*, *cannot yet determine*.
3. For a **UMA query**: follow Section 9.5 — real enquiry, board-endorsed reply, deadline on the clock board.
4. For a **take-over approach** or leak about a transaction: take specialist advice on the SC take-over rules and announcement duties **[V]**.
5. For a **short report**: assess whether any allegations, if true, would be material; consider a measured, factual response via Bursa; avoid personal attacks on the author; consider independent verification of key points.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- Treat any leak signal as the end of the confidentiality window **[C]**.
- Consider **trading halt** if you cannot respond accurately while the market is open (Section 9.4).
- Align every statement with the actual position; avoid statements that could be read as market manipulation or misleading disclosure **[V]**.

**Priority stakeholders:** Bursa/SC; shareholders; analysts; lenders; employees; media.

**Message pillars:** "We take these claims seriously and have reviewed them. Here are the facts we can verify…"; "The Board has commissioned [independent review]"; "We will update…".

**Pitfalls:** blanket denials that later prove wrong; legal threats as the first public response; failure to answer the specific allegations; selective briefing of favoured investors; trading by insiders; buy-backs while in possession of inside information.

**Prepare in advance:** red-team exercise — have someone write the short report on you; document your vulnerabilities and evidence; pre-agreed escalation to the Board; relationships with analysts; templates for UMA replies (T14C).

> **Bold play — "Write the short report yourself."** Once a year, give a small independent team the brief: find the three claims that would hurt us most, using only public information. Fix what is fixable; pre-draft answers to what is not.

---

### S7 — Leadership and governance crisis

**Triggers:** CEO/director misconduct allegations; sudden resignation; death or incapacity of a key leader; boardroom dispute; requisition of an EGM; family or shareholder fight; leaked board papers.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Chair/Lead INED convene the Board; **succession and delegation** plan activated; recusals documented.
2. Disclosure Committee runs materiality screening: resignation of CEO, directors or key management are typically disclosable events **[V]**; contested board or shareholder actions require specialist legal advice.
3. Holding statement (T12) focusing on continuity, governance and next steps.
4. Employees told first by the CEO/Chair where possible; the message is "business continues; here is who leads".

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** director/key management changes and related events; confirm announcement paragraphs and timing **[V]**.
- **Companies Act 2016:** director duties, removal, meeting requisition mechanics **[V]**.
- **Take-over/change in control** triggers if relevant **[V]**.
- **Sensitive personal matters:** health, family matters, allegations — handle with care and legal guidance.

**Priority stakeholders:** employees; Board; major shareholders; lenders; key customers; regulators; media.

**Message pillars:** continuity; board stewardship; respect for individuals; process and timelines; stability of operations.

**Pitfalls:** briefing allied shareholders selectively; leaks from board members; public sparring between directors; the Chair or CEO speaking beyond what the Board has agreed; naming a successor before the Board has actually decided.

**Prepare in advance:** documented succession plan (including emergency); board protocol on confidentiality and leaks; pre-agreed statements for resignation/death; D&O insurance review; understanding of constitutional provisions for board changes.

---

### S8 — Regulatory enforcement, licence action, raids or investigation

**Triggers:** show-cause letter; notice of investigation; search warrant; inspection findings; licence suspension; public reprimand; fine; Bursa Regulation query; SC investigation.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Legal and Company Secretary lead; PR supports. One designated liaison for each authority.
2. Immediately obtain and read the letter or notice; calendar every deadline.
3. Legal hold; privilege review; cooperating but protecting rights.
4. Disclosure Committee: assess materiality and the timing of announcement; check if the authority has requested confidentiality (confirm legal basis and effect on disclosure duties) **[V]**.
5. Holding statement (T11).

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** announce material regulatory actions; consider the **content** carefully — facts, status, company's position, potential impact.
- **Sector regulators:** licence conditions often require notification of enforcement events **[V]**.
- **Cooperation vs. defence:** counsel balances the two; public messaging remains calm and factual.

**Priority stakeholders:** regulators; Board; Bursa/market; lenders; customers affected by licence; employees.

**Message pillars:** cooperation; company's commitment to compliance; summary of measures; how affected customers are protected; next update.

**Pitfalls:** attacking the regulator; premature declarations of "no wrongdoing"; inconsistent messages to different regulators; failing to meet the response deadline.

**Prepare in advance:** regulator engagement protocols; inspection and dawn-raid drills; contact list with regulators; compliance-monitoring reports; pre-drafted holding statements.

---

### S9 — Financial distress: default, liquidity, rating downgrade, PN17, winding-up petition

**Triggers:** covenant breach; missed payment; auditor going-concern language; rating downgrade; supplier payment stops; winding-up demand; statutory demand; PN17/GN3 criteria met.

**First 60 minutes**
1. CFO and Legal map all debt documents, cross-default provisions and notification obligations.
2. Engage restructuring/financial advisers and lawyers; consider approach to lenders **before** a formal default.
3. Disclosure Committee: assess materiality; check **PN17 criteria** (for example equity at or below 25% of share capital and below RM40 million, as reported in a July 2026 classification) **[C/V]**; ACE Market issuers check GN3 **[V]**.
4. Stakeholder messaging for suppliers, employees and customers on continuity; **do not** promise what you cannot deliver.
5. Holding statement and Q&A (T7/T12).

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** announce defaults, demands, petitions and PN17/GN3 triggers promptly and accurately; update as facts change; regularisation plan processes follow classification **[C]**.
- **Lender/trustee notifications** per documents.
- **Ratings:** coordinate with CFO.
- **Insolvency law and creditor rights:** specialist counsel.

**Priority stakeholders:** lenders and trustees; Bursa/market; employees; suppliers and customers; regulators; shareholders.

**Message pillars:** transparency; clear plan; protecting operations and employees; engagement with creditors; timelines.

**Pitfalls:** denying difficulty until the last minute; continuing to issue upbeat messages; paying favoured creditors in ways that raise legal issues; failing to communicate with staff.

**Prepare in advance:** debt map; stress-testing; early-warning indicators; relationship with banks; contingency funding; pre-drafted PN17 communications; legal and financial adviser retainers.

---

### S10 — Litigation, class action, major contract loss

**Triggers:** writ served; shareholder suit; major contract termination; arbitration award; competition or consumer-protection action; adverse judgment.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Legal assesses claim size and likelihood; insurers notified.
2. Disclosure Committee: assess materiality (amount, nature, effect on operations and finances); announce if material **[V — confirm Listing Requirements paragraphs on material litigation]**.
3. Align public statements with litigation strategy; avoid admissions.
4. Holding statement (T11) stating the company's position and that it will vigorously defend or resolve through appropriate channels.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints:** Bursa announcement and ongoing updates for material developments; reflect contingent liabilities and provisions in financial statements.

**Priority stakeholders:** Bursa/market; customers; employees; insurers; lenders.

**Message pillars:** factual summary; company's position; assurance on operations; commitment to update.

**Pitfalls:** public attacks on the claimant; statements that undermine legal position; inconsistency between public disclosure and court filings.

**Prepare in advance:** litigation-communications protocol; Legal–Comms joint review; contract termination playbooks.

---

### S11 — Natural disaster, pandemic, geopolitical or supply-chain disruption

**Triggers:** floods, fire, landslide, haze; pandemic or public-health order; border or port closures; sanctions or tariffs; key supplier failure; war or unrest affecting suppliers/markets.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Safety first; account for staff; shelter and evacuate; BCP activated.
2. Facility/asset damage assessments; supply-chain status.
3. Customer and supplier communications on continuity; internal updates at fixed times.
4. Disclosure Committee: assess whether the event, individually or cumulatively, is material to results, operations or going concern.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints:** Bursa announcements on material business interruption and updates; insurance notifications; authority requirements (for example public-health directives) **[V]**.

**Priority stakeholders:** employees and families; customers; suppliers; authorities; investors; communities.

**Message pillars:** safety; continuity; practical help; transparency about impact and recovery timeline.

**Pitfalls:** minimising the impact; marketing tone-deaf messages; failing to account for staff in East Malaysia or remote sites; neglecting migrant workers' accommodation and welfare.

**Prepare in advance:** BCP and DR plans tested; alternative sites; staff-accounting system; supply-chain visibility; weather/flood watch for exposed sites; pre-approved relief-effort messaging.

---

### S12 — Social-media storm, 3R-sensitive content, boycott, ESG/forced-labour allegations

**Triggers:** viral video or post; offensive advertisement; remarks by staff, endorsers or executives; religious/cultural offence; discrimination claim; NGO or foreign-media report on labour or environmental practices; organised boycott; foreign import restrictions linked to labour allegations.

**First 60 minutes**
1. **Treat any 3R-adjacent matter as Level 3** (Section 7). Senior leadership engaged at once.
2. **Take down** the offending content (preserving a record for Legal) and stop related campaigns.
3. Facts: who made it, when, who approved it, what was meant. Do not guess.
4. Prepare a **sincere, specific apology** (T13) where the company is at fault: acknowledge the offence, state what is wrong, what has been done, and what will change. In BM and English together.
5. Legal assesses possible police reports or regulatory actions (for example under the Communications and Multimedia Act or the Sedition Act) **[V]**. Public members may lodge police reports; MCMC or PDRM may open enquiries.
6. Where relevant, engage community or religious leaders respectfully — and only where doing so is appropriate and not performative.

**Disclosure and regulatory checkpoints**
- **Bursa:** assess materiality where the matter threatens sales, licences, export markets or contracts; ESG/forced-labour allegations that lead to import restrictions or customer terminations can be material **[V]**.
- **Online Safety Act:** report fake or abusive accounts through platform mechanisms **[C]**; do not weaponise takedowns.
- **Employment:** investigate and act on staff conduct through fair process.

**Priority stakeholders:** the offended community; customers; employees; regulators; export customers; investors (for ESG).

**Message pillars:** "We are sorry. This is not who we are. Here is what we have done. Here is what will change. Here is how we will be held to account."

**Pitfalls:** "if anyone was offended" apologies; blaming an intern or agency publicly; deleting evidence; slow response; mixing the apology with a product promotion; using AI-generated translations without human review.

**Prepare in advance:** review process for campaigns (cultural/religious/regional sensitivity review); influencer and endorser contracts with conduct clauses; a standing panel of cultural and religious advisers where appropriate; social media policy; escalation hotline; ESG supply-chain due diligence and grievance mechanisms.

---

### S13 — Misinformation, deepfake, impersonation, fake announcement

**Triggers:** fake "Bursa announcement" or "press release"; doctored video/audio of the CEO or Chair; cloned social accounts offering "investment" or "giveaways"; fake recruitment or customer-service numbers; coordinated rumour campaigns.

**First 60 minutes**
1. Verify authenticity; Comms and CISO analyse the material; preserve evidence.
2. Public clarification on **official channels**: "This is not from us. Official announcements are made only via Bursa LINK and [website]." Link to the correct source.
3. Report to platforms, MCMC and PDRM as advised; Legal considers takedown and injunctive routes **[V]**.
4. Warn customers/shareholders about scams; share the verified hotline.
5. Disclosure Committee: if market-sensitive (for example fake results or fake deal news), consider a **clarification announcement** to Bursa and discuss with Bursa **[V]**.

**Priority stakeholders:** investors and customers vulnerable to scams; employees; regulators; media.

**Message pillars:** clear, calm, directive: "Here is what is genuine. Here is what to ignore. Here is where to report."

**Pitfalls:** amplifying the fake by over-sharing it; slow clarification; relying on one platform; forgetting retail-investor channels.

**Prepare in advance:** verified-channel statement on website and social accounts; domain and handle monitoring; relationships with platform trust-and-safety teams; "How to verify our announcements" page; deepfake response protocol; spokesperson voice/likeness authentication guidance for staff.

## 17. Tabletop exercises

### 17.1 Format

- **Duration:** 2–3 hours for a tabletop; a full-day simulation once a year including the Board.
- **Participants:** Core Five, CMT, Board Chair or ARC Chair, IR, regional heads; observers from outside the CMT.
- **Rules:** play in real roles; use real contact lists and templates; time-box decisions; the facilitator can pause the clock for learning.
- **Evaluation:** score against the criteria in 17.5 and capture actions in the After-action review (T17).
- **Interactive companion:** `docs/crisis-simulator/index.html` (First Sixty) runs three fully branching scenarios based on these exercises, with live regulatory clocks, scoring and a facilitated mode for groups, plus a short academy and the toolkit. It is fictional and carries the same verification limits as this playbook.

### 17.2 Exercise TX1 — "Friday, 6 pm: ransomware on results week"

**Context:** quarterly results are due to be announced on Tuesday (within the two-month deadline). At 6 pm Friday ahead of a long weekend, the finance and ERP systems go down; a ransom note demands payment in 48 hours; a leaked sample contains customer personal data.

| Time | Inject | Discussion |
|---|---|---|
| T+0 | IT reports ransom note | Who is called, in what order? Out-of-band channel? |
| T+30 | CFO: "We can't close the books" | Materiality? Reporting deadline implications? |
| T+60 | Journalist emails with a screenshot of the ransom note | Leak = end of confidentiality? Halt vs. announce? |
| T+120 | Sample customer data on a leak site | PDPA clock; NACSA applicability; customer messaging |
| T+360 | Board member asks about paying the ransom | Who decides? What advice is needed? |
| T+24h | Staff WhatsApp rumour about job losses | Internal comms; leak discipline |
| T+48h | Bursa UMA query as shares fall on Monday | UMA reply protocol |

**Success looks like:** materiality decision logged within 60 minutes; PDPA and Bursa clocks started within 90 minutes; first statement released in EN + BM within 4 hours; ransom decision by the Board; no inconsistencies between Bursa, press and customer messages.

### 17.3 Exercise TX2 — "Pre-open: short-seller report and a UMA query"

**Context:** At 7:30 am a short report alleges revenue inflation. By 9:15 am the stock is down sharply and Bursa issues a UMA query; analysts are calling; WhatsApp and Telegram groups carry screenshots.

| Time | Inject | Discussion |
|---|---|---|
| T+0 | Report published | Who fact-checks which allegation? |
| T+30 | Trading volume spike; analyst calls | Quiet period; IR script |
| T+60 | Bursa UMA query received | Enquiry steps; board endorsement |
| T+120 | Journalist asks for CEO interview | Bursa-first; approach |
| T+240 | Auditor calls: "Are you seeing issues?" | ARC Chair, auditors' role |
| T+24h | Report author issues a follow-up | Update strategy |

**Success looks like:** per-allegation fact-check; UMA reply within the deadline with documented enquiry; consistent public position; no selective briefing; trading restrictions in place; Board decision on independent review.

### 17.4 Exercise TX3 — "Balik kampung weekend: fatality, viral video, and a 3R flashpoint"

**Context:** On the first day of a long festive weekend, a worker dies at a remote Sabah site. A video, taken by a bystander, spreads with a caption making a religious/racial claim about the victim and the company. Families are in another country; the local press is calling; the CEO is overseas.

| Time | Inject | Discussion |
|---|---|---|
| T+0 | Accident reported | HSE and authority notifications; next-of-kin protocol |
| T+45 | Video goes viral | Level; 3R-sensitive; who speaks |
| T+90 | Local politician demands a public statement | Government relations; consistency |
| T+120 | Victim's family in another country has not been told | Embassy, translation, sensitivity |
| T+180 | Bursa announcement needed? | Materiality and halt issues |
| T+24h | Staff WhatsApp groups circulate rumours | Internal comms; leak discipline |
| T+48h | Police report lodged; MCMC enquiry | Legal; cooperation; message |

**Success looks like:** family told before naming; DOSH notified; EN + BM statement within 2 hours; East Malaysia spokesperson active; no blame; no insensitive marketing; documented decision on Bursa disclosure.

### 17.5 Scoring rubric (each 0–3; total / 30)

1. Time to activate CMT · 2. Quality of first statement · 3. Materiality and disclosure decision · 4. Regulatory clocks · 5. Insider controls · 6. One-voice consistency · 7. Language and regional coverage · 8. Stakeholder sequencing · 9. Board involvement · 10. Decision log quality.

## 19. Recovery and after-action review

### 19.1 Exit criteria from crisis mode

Move from crisis to recovery when: (a) people are safe and supported; (b) the situation is contained or stable; (c) required regulatory notifications are done and follow-up schedules agreed; (d) the market has the facts it needs (no pending material announcement); (e) media volume and sentiment have stabilised; (f) the Board agrees. Downgrade level step by step, keeping the Core Five on watch for a defined period.

### 19.2 The 30-60-90 rebuild plan

| Window | Focus | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **0–30 days** | Fix and show | Root cause; interim controls; compensation/remediation under way; independent review commissioned; Board oversight; regular updates |
| **31–60 days** | Prove | Publish findings in appropriate form; implement priority fixes; stakeholder meetings (lenders, major investors, regulators, community); employee engagement |
| **61–90 days** | Embed | Governance and policy changes; training; metrics; independent verification; plan for annual report and sustainability disclosures |

Maintain a **say-do ledger**: every public commitment, owner, due date, status. Review monthly at CMT and quarterly at the Board. Closing commitments on time is the strongest trust signal you control.

### 19.3 Reputation dashboard (weekly in recovery)

| Dimension | Indicators (set baselines before a crisis) |
|---|---|
| Market | Share price vs. FBM KLCI and peers; volume; analyst rating changes; bid–offer behaviour |
| Media | Volume, tone, spokespersons quoted, error rate, East Malaysia coverage |
| Social | Mentions, sentiment, share of misinformation, 3R flags |
| Stakeholders | Customer complaints and churn; supplier terms; lender engagement; regulator correspondence |
| People | Employee pulse score; attrition; whistleblowing volume |
| Governance | Open Board actions; say-do ledger completion |

*Report outcomes, not output: what changed in understanding, trust and behaviour — not how many statements were issued.*

### 19.4 Disclosure and governance follow-through

- Review whether **annual-report disclosures** (risk management and internal control, corporate governance, sustainability) reflect the crisis and the remedial steps.
- Confirm that continuing disclosure obligations (follow-up announcements, quarterly report commentary, contingent liabilities) are covered.
- Review **board effectiveness** and **committee remits** (ARC, risk committee) in light of the crisis.
- Reassess D&O, cyber and other insurance terms.

### 19.5 After-action review and playbook update

Within 30 days of de-escalation, run the After-action review (T17) — psychologically safe, facilitated, focused on system fixes. Present to the Board. Update this playbook, assign owners and dates, and schedule a tabletop to test the changes.
